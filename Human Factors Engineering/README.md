# Analysis of Correlation between Hand Anthropometry and Hand Grip Strength 
# **Project Overview**
<br>
This project investigates the relationship between hand anthropometry and grip strength through the collection and statistical analysis of measurements from approximately 50 university students. The study combines ergonomic hand measurements with grip-strength testing to explore how different hand dimensions relate to the force a person can generate.
<br>
Hand anthropometric measurements were collected using a measuring tape, including hand length, hand breadth, maximum hand breadth, palm length, thumb length, index finger length, middle finger length, ring finger length, and little finger length, along with participant height.
<br>
Grip strength was measured using a hand dynamometer on the right hand at three resistance levels: Level 1 (Easy), Level 2 (Medium), and Level 3 (Hard). These different dynamometer levels were used to observe how grip-strength measurements varied under different resistance settings.
<br>
The collected measurements were organized and analyzed using Microsoft Excel. Statistical analysis included calculating the minimum, maximum, and mean values for the anthropometric and grip-strength measurements. Correlation analysis was then performed to examine the relationship between individual hand dimensions and grip-strength measurements.
<br>
The results were further presented using bar charts and graphical analysis, making it easier to compare the minimum, average, and maximum values across the measured characteristics. The correlation analysis provides insight into the degree and direction of association between hand dimensions and grip strength within the collected dataset.
<br>
Overall, the project demonstrates the application of ergonomics, anthropometric measurement, statistical analysis, correlation analysis, and data visualization to a human-factors engineering problem. The findings can contribute to a better understanding of how variations in hand dimensions are associated with grip performance and can be relevant to the design of hand tools, equipment, workstations, and other products involving manual interaction.
<br><br>
