# Parametric Analysis of Milling using RSM Central Composite Design (CCD) method
# **Project Overview**
<br>
This project focuses on the parametric analysis and optimization of a milling process using Response Surface Methodology (RSM) and Central Composite Design (CCD). The study examines how key machining parameters influence milling performance and uses statistical modeling to identify suitable operating conditions.
<br>
The analysis considers three primary milling parameters: spindle speed, feed rate, and depth of cut. Their effects are investigated against important machining responses, including surface roughness, material removal rate (MRR), and machining time. These responses represent different aspects of milling performance, from product quality and productivity to process efficiency.
<br>
A Central Composite Design (CCD) is used to systematically design the experiments and study both the individual and interaction effects of the selected machining parameters. The experimental data is then analyzed using Response Surface Methodology in Minitab, allowing second-order regression models to be developed for the selected responses.
<br>
The project workflow includes literature review, experimental design, milling experiments, data collection, statistical analysis, regression modeling, response analysis, process optimization, and validation. Response surfaces and statistical results are used to understand how changes in machining parameters affect surface roughness, MRR, and machining time.
<br>
The final stage involves determining machining conditions that provide an appropriate trade-off between productivity, surface quality, and machining efficiency, followed by experimental validation of the optimized parameters.
<br>
Overall, the project demonstrates the application of Design of Experiments (DOE), RSM, CCD, statistical modeling, and process optimization to a real-world manufacturing problem. It provides a systematic approach to understanding milling-process behavior and using experimental data to support data-driven machining decisions.
